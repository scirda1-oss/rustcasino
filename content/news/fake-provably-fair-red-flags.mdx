"Provably fair" is the most common trust badge in Rust gambling — and one of the most abused. Because it *sounds* like a guarantee, sketchier sites paste the label on without building the substance behind it. The good news: a genuine provably-fair system has specific, checkable parts, so you can tell the real thing from decoration in about a minute. Here's how.

## What a real provably-fair system actually has

Four components. If any is missing, the "provably fair" claim is hollow:

- **A pre-committed hash.** Before you bet, the site publishes a **SHA-256 hash of its server seed**. That's the site locking in the outcome in advance — it can't change the result after seeing your bet.
- **The revealed seed afterward.** After the round, the site shows you the **actual server seed**. You hash it yourself; if it matches the hash published earlier, the outcome was fixed before you played.
- **A client seed you control.** You can set or change your own client seed, which mixes into the result. This stops the site from pre-computing outcomes against a value it fully controls.
- **A verifier and documentation.** A legitimate site gives you (or links to) a **verification tool** and a page explaining its exact method — typically SHA-256 or HMAC. Transparency about the algorithm is the whole point.

If you've never done it, our step-by-step [guide to verifying provably fair](/blog/how-to-verify-provably-fair) walks through the actual check.

## Red flags of fake or hollow provably fair

- **The server seed is never revealed.** A hash with no reveal proves nothing — you can't verify a seed you never see.
- **No verification tool.** If there's no way to independently check a result, "provably fair" is just a word.
- **No client-seed control.** If the site controls every input to the randomness, there's nothing stopping manipulation.
- **No documentation — just "trust us."** Reputable sites explain their cryptography; hollow ones wave the badge and change the subject.
- **Results you can't reproduce.** If you run the verification and the numbers don't match, walk away.

## What provably fair does — and doesn't — prove

This is the part even honest sites won't spell out: provably fair proves the **outcome wasn't altered after your bet**. It does **not** prove the odds are generous, that the [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained) is small, or that the site will actually pay your withdrawal. It's **necessary, not sufficient** — a genuine provably-fair game can still carry a steep edge and a slow payout process.

## Bottom line

Treat "provably fair" as a claim to test, not a promise to trust. Verify one result yourself the first time you use a site; if it can't be verified — no revealed seed, no tool, no docs — the badge is decoration, and that tells you plenty about the operator.
