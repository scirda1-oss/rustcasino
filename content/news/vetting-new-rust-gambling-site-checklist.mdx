The Rust gambling space never sits still — new sites, rebrands, and outright clones appear constantly. A polished homepage and a flashy welcome bonus tell you nothing about whether a site will actually pay you. Before you deposit on one you've never heard of, run it through this checklist. It won't guarantee safety — nothing does on an unregulated site — but it filters out most of the bad ones.

## The checklist

- **Verify the provably fair yourself.** Don't trust the badge — check a result. A real system publishes a hashed server seed before the round, reveals the seed after, lets you set a client seed, and gives you a verifier. If you can't reproduce a result, stop. (See [fake provably-fair red flags](/fake-provably-fair-red-flags).)
- **Read the published terms.** Fees, withdrawal methods, KYC thresholds, and [bonus wagering](/rust-gambling-bonus-wagering-requirements) should all be findable and clear. Vague, buried, or contradictory terms are a red flag.
- **Check independent reputation.** Look at recent community feedback — the site's Discord, its subreddit, review platforms — reading the *newest* comments, not just an overall score. A brand-new site with no track record isn't automatically bad, but it's unproven, so treat it that way.
- **Look for responsible-gambling tools.** Even a basic [self-ban or cool-off](/responsible-gambling-tools-rust-sites) signals a more serious operator; a total absence signals the opposite.
- **Test support before depositing.** Send a question. Responsive, coherent support is a good sign; silence or canned nonsense is not.
- **Confirm ownership isn't a black box.** Vague or hidden operator information is a common thread in sites that later vanish with balances.

## The one test that beats all the others

Here's the single most useful thing you can do with an unproven site: **start tiny and complete the full loop.** Deposit a small amount, play a little, and — the crucial step — **withdraw**. A site that pays a small withdrawal cleanly and promptly has passed the test that actually matters. One that stalls, adds surprise verification, or "loses" a $5 payout has told you everything you need to know **before** you risked anything real.

Never scale up on a new site until it has paid you out at least once, smoothly.

## Keep your account safe throughout

None of this matters if your account gets phished. Only log in on the real Steam domains, verify the exact domain (clones use lookalike URLs), never authenticate through a link someone sent you, and guard your Steam Web API key.

No regulator is vetting these sites for you — that job [falls to you](/is-rust-skin-gambling-against-steam-rules).
