You had a good session, hit withdraw, and now there are skins in your Steam inventory. Here's the part that catches people out: **that isn't money yet.** A Rust skin, and even Steam Wallet credit, is not cash in your bank — and every route from "site balance" to "real money" shaves a bit off along the way. Here's how the cash-out actually works.

## First, the hard fact about Steam credit

You *can* sell skins on the **Steam Community Market** — but the proceeds land in your **Steam Wallet**, and Steam Wallet credit has **no cash value outside Steam**. Valve's own Subscriber Agreement is explicit: there's no official way to withdraw Steam Wallet balance to a bank, PayPal, or card. So the Steam market is great if you just want more games or other skins — and useless if your goal is money out.

That leaves two real routes to actual cash.

## Route 1: Crypto withdrawal (cleanest, if supported)

If the site supports **crypto cash-out**, it's the most direct path: withdraw crypto from the site, then convert it to cash through an exchange. It sidesteps the skin-selling step entirely. Watch for the usual friction — **level gates** on new accounts, **KYC** on larger amounts, and withdrawal fees — which we break down in [crypto vs. skin deposits](/crypto-vs-skin-deposits-rust-gambling).

## Route 2: Skins → a third-party marketplace (real cash, at a cost)

To turn skins themselves into real money, you sell them on a **reputable third-party skin marketplace or cash-out service**, which pays out via PayPal, bank transfer, card, or crypto. Two things to know:

- **Instant sells pay less; patient P2P listings pay more.** Selling to the platform instantly is fast but takes a lower return; listing for another buyer can fetch more, especially on rarer items, but is slower.
- **Vet the marketplace like a gambling site.** Clone login pages and fake middlemen are active threats — never log in through a link someone sent you, and guard your **Steam Web API key** (see [the API-key scam](/steam-api-key-phishing-rust-gambling)).

## The honest math: every hop takes a cut

This is the bit worth internalising. A realistic cash-out chain looks like: **site withdrawal fee → skin conversion spread → marketplace fee.** Stack those and the money that reaches your bank is meaningfully less than the number you saw on the site. Real cash-out is rarely one-to-one — budget for the shrinkage rather than being surprised by it.

## Bottom line

- **Want cash out cleanly?** Crypto, if the site offers it.
- **Selling skins?** The Steam market only gives you Steam credit; a **vetted third-party marketplace** gives real money, minus fees.
- **Either way:** complete KYC early if the amount is sizeable, and never rush a trade — the pressure to "verify quickly" is exactly where scams live. If a [withdrawal stalls](/rust-gambling-withdrawal-stuck-what-to-do), we've covered what recourse you actually have.

Getting money out of the skin economy is very doable — just go in knowing it costs a few percent at each step, and that "Steam credit" and "cash" are not the same thing.
