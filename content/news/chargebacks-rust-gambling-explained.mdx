When a session goes badly, a tempting thought shows up: *"I'll just chargeback the deposit and get my money back."* It sounds like a safety net. It isn't — that's not what a chargeback is for, and trying it that way usually makes things worse. Here's the honest picture.

## What a chargeback actually is

A chargeback (a card dispute) is a consumer-protection tool for **fraud or a service not delivered** — money taken without your consent, or a product you paid for and never received. It is **not** a refund button for a purchase you regret. Banks and card networks routinely **decline** disputes that amount to "I gambled and lost," because losing a bet you chose to place isn't fraud.

## Where a chargeback legitimately applies

There are narrow, real cases:

- **Unauthorised charges.** Someone used your card without permission and you never authorised the deposit. That's genuine fraud and a valid dispute.
- **A true service failure.** You deposited by **card**, the site never credited your balance, and support won't resolve it. That's arguably a service not delivered — the legitimate lane for a dispute.

If you're in one of these, act promptly: card disputes have time limits, commonly around **60–120 days**, and you'll need evidence — transaction records, timestamps, support messages.

## Where it does not apply

- **Recovering gambling losses.** Deposited, played, lost? That's not a valid basis. It's buyer's remorse, and it gets declined.
- **Crypto and skin deposits.** There is **no chargeback mechanism** for these. Once crypto or skins leave your control, they're gone — one more reason to understand [crypto vs. skin deposits](/crypto-vs-skin-deposits-rust-gambling) before funding.

## Why misusing it backfires

Filing a chargeback to claw back *losses* — sometimes called "friendly fraud" — carries real consequences. Gambling sites treat it as exactly that: your account can be **banned and your details blacklisted** across operators that share fraud data, and repeated disputes can flag you with your **bank or payment processor**, affecting future card use. A chargeback is not a free reset — it's a formal fraud claim, and using it dishonestly has a cost.

## The honest bottom line

Chargebacks are a backstop for **fraud and undelivered service**, not a way to un-lose a bet. On an unregulated Rust site, your real protection was never the dispute process — it's **prevention**: read the terms first, keep deposits to what you can lose, and know [what to do if a withdrawal genuinely stalls](/rust-gambling-withdrawal-stuck-what-to-do) (which is a service issue, not a loss).

And if you find yourself reaching for a chargeback to recover money you gambled away, treat that as the signal it is — a good moment to step back. Our [responsible gambling](/responsible-gambling) page has support resources, and a [budget you can afford to lose](/rust-gambling-bankroll-basics) is the only real safety net here.
