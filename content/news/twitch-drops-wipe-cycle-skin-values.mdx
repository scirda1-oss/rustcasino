Every skin deposit is really a deposit of **market value**, and that value isn't static — it moves. Two recurring cycles do most of the moving in Rust: **Twitch Drops** and the **monthly wipe**. If you gamble with skins, understanding them helps you avoid quietly under-pricing your own deposit.

## Twitch Drops: the supply-surge cycle

Twitch Drops are time-limited skins you claim by watching Rust streams during a campaign. Because thousands of players claim the same items in the same window, drops follow a fairly predictable price arc:

- **During the campaign,** supply surges and prices for those drop items typically **dip** — everyone has them at once.
- **After it ends,** supply is fixed. Over the following months to years, natural attrition (items sitting locked in inventories, abandoned accounts) slowly shrinks the circulating count, and prices for desirable drops often **appreciate**.

The practical read: a freshly-dropped skin is usually near its **cheapest** right after the campaign, while the same item held long-term can be worth considerably more. Scarcity meeting steady demand is the engine, as we covered in [what makes a skin valuable](/what-makes-rust-skins-valuable).

## The monthly wipe cycle

Rust runs a **forced monthly wipe** (traditionally the first Thursday of the month), when servers reset and players start fresh. Activity — and attention on certain skins as people "kit up" for a new wipe — tends to cluster around these windows. It's a rhythm worth being aware of, because demand and trading interest aren't evenly spread across the month.

## Why gamblers should care

Here's the link back to the deposit screen: a site credits your skin deposit at **current market value** (usually a bit below Steam's, given the [conversion spread](/cash-out-rust-winnings-real-money)). So:

- **Depositing a freshly-dropped skin during a supply surge** means crediting it near its price floor — you may be handing over value you'd get more for later.
- **Gambling away a retired, appreciating drop** means potentially forfeiting an item on an upward trajectory, not just "a skin."

The fix is simple: **check the current market value before you convert**, and recognise that "just a skin" might be an item that's rising.

## The honest caution

Don't twist this into a "strategy." You **cannot** beat the [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained) by timing your deposits around drop cycles or wipes — the games don't care when you funded. This is purely about not **under-valuing** your own deposit or gambling away an appreciating asset by accident. It affects what you're putting in, not your odds once you play.

## Bottom line

Skin value breathes with Twitch Drop campaigns and the monthly wipe. Knowing roughly where a skin sits in those cycles helps you deposit at a fair value instead of a low one — and reminds you that some skins are worth more held than staked.
