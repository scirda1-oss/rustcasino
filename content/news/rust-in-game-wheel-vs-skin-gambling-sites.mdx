Ask two Rust players about "gambling in Rust" and you can get two completely different answers. One is talking about the wheel at Bandit Camp; the other is talking about a website where real skins change hands. They're worlds apart in risk, and mixing them up is how people get caught out. Here's the clean version.

## The in-game wheel: scrap, and only scrap

Rust has a gambling wheel built into the game itself, found in the safe-zone at **Bandit Camp**. You walk up, place a bet in **scrap** — the in-game crafting currency — on a colour/multiplier, and the wheel spins. Win and you get more scrap; lose and it's gone.

The key point: this is **entirely inside the game**. Scrap has no cash value, you can't withdraw it, and you can't top it up with a credit card. It's a gameplay mechanic, the same as any other risk you take on a wipe. Nobody is going to KYC you for spinning the Bandit Camp wheel.

## Skin gambling sites: real value, real risk

Third-party **skin gambling sites** are a different animal. These are websites — not part of Rust or Facepunch — where you deposit **Rust skins** (or crypto) that carry real-world value, because Rust skins trade on the [Steam Community Market](https://steamcommunity.com/market/). You play case openings, coinflips, crash, jackpot and so on, then withdraw skins or crypto.

Because real value is moving, everything that makes real gambling risky applies:

- **Your deposit is worth actual money.** Losing a skin balance is losing money, not scrap.
- **Payouts aren't guaranteed the way scrap is.** Sites can gate withdrawals behind KYC, wagering requirements or account levels.
- **The operator is a company you're trusting**, not a game mechanic. Some are reputable; some are not.
- **"Provably fair" only exists here** — and even then it only proves a result wasn't altered, not that the odds or the operator are good. If you use these sites, learn to [verify a provably fair result](/blog/how-to-verify-provably-fair) yourself.

## Why the distinction actually matters

Two reasons. First, **age and money**: the Bandit Camp wheel is a game feature, but skin gambling sites are real-money gambling and are 18+ (older, depending on your jurisdiction). Treating one like the other is exactly how a teenager ends up depositing a skin inventory they can't afford to lose.

Second, **language games**. Some sites lean on the familiarity of the in-game wheel to make real-money gambling feel like "just part of Rust." It isn't. The moment a real skin leaves your Steam inventory into a third-party site, you've stepped outside the game and into gambling with real stakes.

## The takeaway

- **Bandit Camp wheel** = in-game, scrap only, no cash value, no withdrawals. A game mechanic.
- **Skin gambling sites** = external websites, real skins/crypto, real losses, real withdrawal terms. Real gambling.

If you only ever spin the in-game wheel, you're playing Rust. If you're depositing skins on a website, you're gambling — so treat it that way: know [how it actually works](/blog/how-rust-skin-gambling-works), stick to sites you've checked, and never stake more than you'd be fine losing.
