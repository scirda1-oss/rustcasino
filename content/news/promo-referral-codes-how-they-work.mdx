Every Rust gambling site pushes promo codes, and every review site (including this one) publishes them. It's worth understanding what's actually happening when you enter one — because a code is a **two-sided deal**, and knowing both sides helps you use them without being quietly steered.

## A code does two things at once

"Promo code," "referral code," and "affiliate code" are, on most sites, the same thing. When you enter one at sign-up, it typically:

- **Rewards you** — free coins, a free case, a deposit match, or rakeback.
- **Credits whoever owns the code** — the creator, streamer, or site that gave it to you.

Both happen from the same code. You get a welcome perk; someone else gets you as a referral.

## Who earns what — and why codes are everywhere

Here's the part that explains the whole ecosystem: the code owner usually earns an **ongoing cut** — commonly a percentage of what their referrals **wager over time**, paid out as commission or credits, not a single one-off reward. That recurring income is exactly why codes are promoted so relentlessly across YouTube, Discord and review sites.

Crucially, **it doesn't cost you more.** Your bonus comes from the site's marketing budget — which, like everything, is ultimately funded by the [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained) — not added to your deposit. When the site is good, it's a genuine win-win: you get a bonus, the referrer gets paid, and you don't pay extra.

## Why the incentive should matter to you

The catch isn't cost — it's **bias**. Anyone promoting a code has a financial reason to. A good affiliate still recommends sites on merit and discloses the relationship; a bad one promotes whoever pays the most, loudest. So make your decision on **fundamentals** — verifiable [provably fair](/fake-provably-fair-red-flags), clear terms, a real payout track record — not on how enthusiastically a code is pushed.
