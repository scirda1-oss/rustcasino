"Is a Rust gambling site basically an online casino?" It's a reasonable question — they both let you bet on game outcomes — but the honest answer is that they're **structurally different products**. Mistaking one for the other is where people get burned, so here's the plain side-by-side.

## Currency

- **Regulated casino:** fiat money — you deposit and withdraw in dollars, euros, etc., to a bank or card.
- **Skin site:** Rust **skins** (and sometimes crypto) are the value. You're moving items, not money, with a [conversion spread](/what-makes-rust-skins-valuable) at each step.

## Regulation and protection

This is the biggest gap:

- **Regulated casino:** holds a **licence number**, answers to a gambling **regulator** you can complain to, is usually covered by an independent **dispute resolver** (an eCOGRA-style ADR), and must provide mandated **responsible-gambling tools** and KYC. If it withholds a payout, you have somewhere to escalate.
- **Skin site:** typically **unlicensed**, with **no regulator, no independent ADR**, [thin RG tooling](/responsible-gambling-tools-rust-sites), and often opaque ownership. If a payout [stalls](/rust-gambling-withdrawal-stuck-what-to-do), your recourse is the operator's support plus community pressure — that's it.

## How fairness is proven

Two different models:

- **Regulated casino:** independent labs **audit the RTP** — a third party verifies the house's odds over time.
- **Skin site:** **[provably fair](/fake-provably-fair-red-flags)** — you verify individual results yourself, per round.

Both have value, but they're not the same guarantee. Provably fair proves a specific round wasn't altered; it isn't an RTP audit and it says nothing about the operator's solvency or willingness to pay.

## The games

They overlap on **crash, coinflip, jackpot and roulette**. Regulated casinos add licensed **slots and live dealer** tables; skin sites add **case openings, case battles and the upgrader**, all built around the skins economy. Different libraries, same underlying [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained).

## Which one suits you

- Want **cash in and out, a visible licence number, a regulator's backstop, and audited games**? That's a **regulated casino** — and a Rust skin site is simply not that product, no matter how polished it looks.
- Want to bet **skins inside the Rust ecosystem**, with **per-round provably-fair transparency**, and you accept the unregulated trade-offs? That's what **skin gambling** is.

## Bottom line

Neither is "better" in the abstract — they're different products with different protections. The costly mistake is treating a skin site like a regulated casino and expecting safeguards that aren't there: no regulator, no ADR, no audited RTP, no guaranteed cash-out.
