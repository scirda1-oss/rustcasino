Two thoughts run through almost every Rust gambling session: *"I've lost a bunch in a row — I'm due for a win,"* and its mirror image, *"I'm on a heater, let it ride."* They feel like opposite instincts. On a random game, they're the **exact same error** — and it's the single most expensive one you can make.

## Why "I'm due" is a myth

The gambler's fallacy is the belief that past results change the odds of the next **independent** outcome. They don't. A [provably-fair](/fake-provably-fair-red-flags) RNG has no memory of your streak. After five losses on the wheel, crash, or a coinflip, the next result's odds are **exactly what they always were** — the game doesn't owe you a win to "balance things out."

The confusion comes from mixing two different things: over an enormous number of trials, results trend toward the long-run average — but that says nothing about your *next* spin. There's no cosmic accountant nudging a red because you've seen five blacks. Each event stands alone.

## Why "I'm hot" is the same mistake, inverted

The hot-hand version feels smarter, but on a random game it's just as wrong. A wheel can't be "hot." An RNG has no **momentum**. A run of wins doesn't make the next bet more likely to land — it just makes *you* feel invincible and bet bigger at precisely the worst moment. Interestingly, research finds people flip between the two: they expect a reversal after *short* streaks (gambler's fallacy) and expect continuation after *long* ones (hot-hand) — both illusions, both from the same failure to accept that random is random.

## Where it actually hurts you

This isn't abstract. The fallacy is the **engine behind chasing losses** ("I'm due, so I'll bet more to get it back") *and* behind letting winnings ride ("I'm hot, so I'll keep going"). Both push your bet size up at exactly the wrong time, and both are made worse by [near-miss design](/how-rust-gambling-sites-keep-you-playing) that whispers "so close, go again." Combine a false sense of "due" with a rising bet size and you have the classic recipe for a bankroll evaporating in minutes.

## How to inoculate yourself

- **Treat every bet as its own event** with fixed odds. A streak is a pattern your brain invents after the fact, not a signal about the future.
- **Pre-set your bet size and stop-loss/stop-win** so a streak — hot or cold — can't change how much you wager. This is the whole point of a [bankroll plan](/rust-gambling-bankroll-basics).
- **Verify one provably-fair result** to internalise, concretely, that outcomes are independent and unpredictable.

## Bottom line

The wheel has no memory, and neither does the RNG. "I'm due" and "I'm hot" are two faces of the same illusion, and believing either one changes only one thing: how much you bet, at the worst possible moment. You can't out-guess randomness — but you can refuse to let a streak, in either direction, touch your bet size. That refusal is the actual skill.
