You won, you hit withdraw, and… nothing. A pending withdrawal is one of the most stressful moments in skin gambling, partly because it's genuinely ambiguous: it might be a routine hold, or it might be a site that has no intention of paying. Here's how to tell the difference and what you can actually do.

## First: delay or stall?

Plenty of holds are **normal and clear on their own**:

- **KYC on larger amounts.** Bigger cash-outs often trigger [identity verification](/kyc-on-rust-gambling-sites-explained) — expected, and it resolves once you verify.
- **Withdrawal review or batching.** Some sites manually review or process in batches, so it isn't instant.
- **Redeem cooldowns.** A few sites limit how often you can pull skins.

A **stall**, by contrast, shows a pattern. Treat these as red flags, especially together:

- Support won't tell you the actual reason for the hold.
- **New verification demands appear each time** you satisfy the last one.
- Approved withdrawals get repeatedly cancelled.
- You're nudged to **reverse the withdrawal and gamble it**.
- Account access disappears right after you request payment.
- Recent, independent complaints describe the same thing happening to others.

## What you can actually do

Your options are limited but real — work them in order:

- **Preserve evidence first.** Screenshot your balance, bet history, the withdrawal request with timestamps, and every support message. Do this before anything can be edited or your access changes.
- **Escalate calmly, in writing.** Contact official support, reference the site's own published withdrawal terms, and keep it factual. A clear paper trail is your strongest asset.
- **Use community pressure.** Posting the situation on the site's Discord, its subreddit, or a review platform sometimes moves a stuck payout — operators care about public reputation.
- **Chargeback only applies to card/fiat deposits.** If you funded with a credit or debit card, your bank may reverse it. **Crypto and skin deposits generally can't be charged back**, so this route often isn't available.

## The hard truth about recourse

This is the part worth internalising: most Rust gambling sites are **unlicensed and unregulated**. There's no gambling authority to file a complaint with and no independent dispute resolver (no eCOGRA-style ADR). Your recourse is the operator's own support process, community pressure, and — for fiat only — a chargeback. That's the whole list.

Which is exactly why the real protection happens **before** you deposit.

## Prevention beats disputes

- **Complete KYC early** if you plan to cash out anything sizeable, so it isn't a surprise later.
- **Read the withdrawal terms before depositing**, not after you win.
- **Stick to sites with a payout track record** — our [legit checks](/legit) and [reviews](/best-rust-gambling-sites) exist so you're not the one field-testing whether a site pays.
- **Never stake more than you'd be fine losing**, because a stuck withdrawal is always a possibility on an unregulated site.

A held withdrawal isn't automatically a scam — but on an unlicensed site, you're your own regulator. Document everything, escalate cleanly, and choose vetted operators so the situation is far less likely in the first place.
