Every skin deposit on a Rust gambling site is really a deposit of **market value** — the site credits you based on what your item is worth. So understanding what drives that value isn't trivia; it's how you judge whether a conversion rate is fair. Here's what actually moves Rust skin prices.

## Rust's market works differently from CS2

If you've come from CS2, drop those instincts. Rust skins have **no float/wear values** and **no stamped rarity grades** on each item. There's no "Factory New vs Battle-Scarred" and no colour-coded tier deciding a price. Value is set almost entirely by two things: **scarcity and demand** at a given moment. That makes the market simpler to understand but more volatile — a skin is worth what someone will pay for it today, full stop.

## What actually drives value

A handful of durable factors do most of the work:

- **Discontinuation.** Facepunch rotates the in-game store, and once a skin stops being sold, its supply is effectively fixed. Limited-run items that never return tend to hold value best.
- **Age.** Older skins from early store rotations often carry a premium simply because no more are being created while new players keep joining and wanting them.
- **Design appeal.** Clean, distinctive designs hold value better than busy or generic ones — taste matters in a market with no rarity tiers to lean on.
- **Special-drop scarcity.** Skins from past Twitch drop campaigns have a hard-capped supply once the campaign ends, which is why some become sought-after long after.

The common thread is **fixed or shrinking supply meeting ongoing demand.** That's what turns a cosmetic into a store of value.

## Why this matters at the deposit screen

Two practical takeaways for gambling:

- **Value floats, so check it fresh.** A skin's worth isn't a fixed number — it moves with demand. Look up the current [Steam Community Market](https://steamcommunity.com/market/) price before you deposit, not last month's.
- **Mind the conversion spread.** Sites credit deposits at *their* valuation, which is frequently **below** market value. Knowing what your skin actually trades for is the only way to spot a poor conversion rate — a point we dig into in [crypto vs. skin deposits](/crypto-vs-skin-deposits-rust-gambling).

## The bottom line

Rust skin value is pure supply and demand: scarce, discontinued, aged or well-designed items hold up; common ones don't. Before you convert any skin into site balance, know roughly what it's worth on the open market — because the moment it becomes "balance," you've accepted whatever rate the site offered.
