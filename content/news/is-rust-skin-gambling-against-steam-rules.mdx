A question that comes up constantly: are Rust skin gambling sites "official," and can using them get your Steam account in trouble? It's worth answering plainly, because a lot of players assume these sites are part of the ecosystem in a way they simply aren't.

## These sites are third-party — full stop

Rust skin gambling sites are **not** run, endorsed, or operated by **Facepunch** (Rust's developer) or **Valve** (which runs Steam). They're independent businesses built *on top of* the Steam item economy — they use Steam trading to move skins in and out, but they aren't authorized partners of it. When a site displays Rust skins, that's the third party plugging into Steam's trading system, not Steam blessing the site.

That distinction matters the moment something goes wrong: because these operators aren't affiliated with Valve or Facepunch, **there's no official backstop.** Steam support won't reverse a gambling loss or recover skins you sent to a third-party site.

## What Steam's rules actually restrict

Steam's Subscriber Agreement and API terms restrict using your account, your items, and the **Steam Web API** to run or facilitate third-party gambling. Valve has long taken the position that it does **not** authorize skin-gambling sites, and it has acted against them and against accounts tied to that activity over the years. Scrutiny of skin gambling — from Valve and from regulators generally — has only increased, not eased.

The practical upshot: engaging with these sites sits in a **grey area** that runs against the spirit, and often the letter, of Steam's rules. That doesn't mean casual use guarantees a ban, but it does mean you're operating outside anything Valve supports or protects.

## What this means for you

You don't need to panic, but you should be clear-eyed:

- **There's no safety net.** No affiliation means no official recourse — Steam won't help if a site withholds a withdrawal or an item vanishes.
- **Your account is the asset to protect.** Guard your login and especially your **Steam Web API key**, which is the exact thing skin-phishing scams target — see [the API-key scam](/steam-api-key-phishing-rust-gambling).
- **"Unregulated" cuts both ways.** These sites aren't overseen by a gambling regulator either, so player protections you'd expect from a licensed casino largely don't exist.

## The takeaway

Treat Rust skin gambling for what it is: **unofficial, unaffiliated, and unregulated** third-party activity built on Steam's economy, not a sanctioned part of it. That's not a reason to moralise — it's a reason to protect your account, keep your API key clean, and stick to sites you've actually vetted, like the ones we put through [legit checks](/legit). If you understand there's no official backstop, you'll make calmer decisions about what you're willing to risk.
