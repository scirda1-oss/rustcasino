Crash is one of the most addictive formats on Rust sites, and one of the most misread. A line climbs, a multiplier ticks up, and you cash out before it "crashes." It *feels* like a game of skill and nerve. Underneath, it's a clean piece of probability — and understanding it is the difference between playing it with clear eyes and falling for a "system."

## How crash works

Each round, a multiplier starts at **1.00x** and climbs an accelerating curve. At a random point, it **crashes**. If you cash out before the crash, you win your bet times the multiplier you cashed at. If the crash comes first, you lose that bet. Rounds are quick — seconds long — with a short window to place your bet before the next one starts.

That's the whole game: pick when to jump off a rising line that will fall at an unknown moment.

## What auto-cashout actually does

**Auto-cashout** lets you set a target multiplier — say 1.5x or 2x — and the system cashes you out automatically the instant it's hit. It does two useful things:

- **Removes emotion.** No freezing, no greed-driven "just a bit higher."
- **Removes latency risk.** A manual click can land *after* the crash if your connection stutters; an auto-cashout instruction is registered before the round, so timing isn't at the mercy of your ping.

What it does **not** do is change the odds. It just executes your chosen exit reliably. Consistent, not magic.

## Provably fair — what it proves

The crash point for each round is **committed before the round begins**: the site generates a server seed, publishes its hash up front, and can't move the crash point based on how much is wagered or when people cash out. You can [verify a result](/blog/how-to-verify-provably-fair) afterward. That proves the round wasn't rigged — it does **not** mean the game is beatable.

## The myth of a system

Here's the part that costs people money. Crash rounds are **mathematically independent**. No streak is "due," no multiplier is "overdue," and last round tells you nothing about the next. That means:

- **Martingale (doubling after a loss) doesn't beat the edge.** It just trades many small wins for the occasional catastrophic losing run that wipes a bankroll or hits a bet limit.
- **No "predictor" app or pattern works.** The crash point is set by a provably fair RNG. Anyone selling a tool that reads the graph to forecast crashes is selling a scam.

## The honest maths

The [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained) is baked into the crash distribution — a small chance of an instant 1.00x bust, and the curve shifted slightly toward the house — and it's typically in the low single digits. Cashing out at low multipliers gives frequent small wins and rare busts; aiming high gives rare big wins. **Neither beats the edge over time.** It just changes how the same long-run result feels.
