"Deposit and we'll double it." It's the most common promo in Rust gambling, and it sounds like free money. It isn't — not yet. Bonus coins come attached to **wagering requirements**, and until you understand those, the headline offer tells you almost nothing about what you can actually withdraw.

## What a wagering requirement is

A wagering requirement is how many times you have to **bet** a bonus before the site lets you cash any of it out. It's usually written as a multiplier — you'll see things like "30x."

Take an illustrative example. Say you get a **$50 bonus with 30x wagering**. Before you can withdraw, you have to place **$50 × 30 = $1,500** in total bets. You don't need $1,500 — the same coins get wagered over and over as you win and lose — but you do have to run that volume through the games first. On some sites the requirement applies to the **bonus plus your deposit**, which makes the number bigger still.

That's the gap the headline hides: a "$50 bonus" is not $50 you can take out. It's $50 of play with strings attached.

## The details that change the math

Two bonuses with the same headline can be worlds apart. Before you claim one, check:

- **What the multiplier applies to** — bonus only, or bonus + deposit.
- **Game weighting.** Some games contribute 100% toward the requirement, others far less, so grinding it out on the "wrong" game can stall you.
- **Expiry.** Bonuses commonly expire (30 days is typical). Miss the window and the bonus — and sometimes the winnings from it — are forfeited.
- **Max cashout.** Some bonuses cap how much you're allowed to withdraw from them, no matter how well you run.

None of these are hidden if you read the terms. All of them are easy to miss if you don't.

This is exactly why we keep pointing at **rakeback** over headline bonuses. Rakeback returns a slice of the house edge you generate back to you, usually with **no wagering strings** — it's yours to withdraw. A flashy "100% match" is locked behind playthrough; a steady rakeback schedule quietly pays out. Over any real number of sessions, the boring option is usually worth more. ## Before you claim any bonus

- **Read the wagering multiplier** and whether it hits bonus-only or bonus + deposit.
- **Check game weighting** so you're playing what actually counts.
- **Note the expiry and any max-cashout cap.**
- **Never deposit more than you'd be fine losing** just to "unlock" an offer. A bonus is a reason to play a site, not a reason to overspend.
