People lump every "skin site" into one bucket, but there are two fundamentally different things happening out there, and confusing them leads to bad decisions. One is a **bet**; the other is a **transaction**. Knowing which you're doing changes how you should think about the risk entirely.

## Skin trading and marketplaces: a transaction

Skin **trading** means exchanging or selling items for their value in an organised marketplace. You're not playing a game — you're doing a deal at a price. It comes in a few flavours:

- **Steam trades with other players** — free, but slow and manual.
- **Bot trading sites** — instant swaps, priced on a spread (you accept a slightly worse rate for speed).
- **Third-party marketplaces** — often the best value for cashing out, but slower.

The defining feature: you know roughly **what you're getting before you commit**. There's a fee or spread, but the outcome isn't random. This is how you turn skins into other skins, or into money — see our [cash-out guide](/cash-out-rust-winnings-real-money).

## Skin gambling: a bet

Skin **gambling** means wagering skins or balance on **games of chance** — coinflip, jackpot, case openings, crash, case battles. The outcome is random, the [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained) means you lose over time, and your stake can go to **zero by design**. That's not a flaw; it's what gambling is.

## Why the distinction actually matters

The risk profiles are completely different:

- **A trade** exchanges value for value, minus a fee. You end the transaction with *something* close to what you put in.
- **A bet** can wipe your stake entirely. You might end with nothing.

Treating a bet like a "sure" cash-out — or assuming a gambling site is just a marketplace — is how people lose money they thought was safe. And the line gets deliberately blurred: **case opening** is gambling, not "buying a skin," and the [upgrader](/rust-upgrader-game-explained) is gambling dressed up as "trading up." Some platforms do both, so read what you're actually clicking.

## The one-question test

Ask: **is the outcome random, or a fixed exchange at a price?**

- If you can **lose your stake to chance**, it's gambling — apply a budget and treat it as paid entertainment.
- If it's an **exchange at a known-ish rate**, it's trading — judge it on fees, speed, and the marketplace's trustworthiness.

## Both share one risk

Whichever you're doing, the same threat sits underneath: **phishing and API-key theft.** Fake login pages and "verify on this link" scams target traders and gamblers alike, so guard your account and never authenticate through a stranger's link (see [the API-key scam](/steam-api-key-phishing-rust-gambling)).

## Bottom line

Trading is a transaction; gambling is a bet. One returns value minus a fee; the other can return nothing.
