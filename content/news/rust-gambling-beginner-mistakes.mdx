Most of what goes wrong for new Rust gamblers isn't bad luck — it's a small set of avoidable mistakes made in the first ten minutes. Get these five right and you've dodged the majority of first-timer regret. None of them require being an expert.

## 1. Ignoring the skin conversion spread

When you deposit a skin, the site credits you at *its* valuation — often **below** the Steam market price. New players skip this and effectively pay a hidden fee before they've even played. Look up what your skin actually trades for first (see [what makes a skin valuable](/what-makes-rust-skins-valuable)), and weigh whether depositing skins or [crypto](/crypto-vs-skin-deposits-rust-gambling) costs you less on that particular site.

## 2. Chasing a big bonus without reading the wagering

"Deposit and we'll double it" is the loudest offer and the most misunderstood. A bonus almost always carries **wagering requirements** — you have to bet it many times over before you can withdraw a cent. A smaller offer with light terms can beat a huge one locked behind heavy playthrough. Always read the [wagering requirements](/rust-gambling-bonus-wagering-requirements) before you claim.

## 3. Leaving skins parked on the site

Balances sitting on a site aren't in your control. Sites can go down, accounts can be locked, and withdrawals can hit KYC holds. The fix is boring and effective: **withdraw what you're not actively playing**, and read the withdrawal terms *before* you deposit, not after you win — our guide on a [stuck withdrawal](/rust-gambling-withdrawal-stuck-what-to-do) covers what recourse you actually have (spoiler: not much, on an unregulated site).

## 4. Believing in a "system" and chasing losses

Every game on these sites carries a [house edge](/house-edge-on-rust-gambling-explained), and no betting pattern beats it. Doubling after a loss (martingale) doesn't work — it just sets up the occasional catastrophic losing run. Rounds are independent; no result is "due." If you're down, adding more money to "win it back" is the single most reliable way to lose more. When it stops being fun, stop.

## 5. Logging in through a link someone sent you

The fastest way to lose an *entire* inventory isn't a coinflip — it's a **fake login page**. Someone DMs you a link ("vote for my team," "claim a reward"), you log into a lookalike Steam page, and they drain your account or steal your API key. Only log in on the real Steam domains, bookmark the sites you use, and never authenticate through a stranger's link — details in [the Steam API-key scam](/steam-api-key-phishing-rust-gambling).

## The one habit under all five

Set a number you'd be genuinely fine losing **before** you start, and treat the whole thing as paid entertainment on an unregulated platform — because that's exactly what it is. Do that, avoid the five above, and you've skipped most of what turns a fun session into an expensive lesson.
